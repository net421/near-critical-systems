TITLE
Reliability-Based Threshold Control for Near-Critical Capacity-Constrained Systems: Corrected First-Passage Dynamics and Hazard Characterization

SHORT TITLE
Reliability-Based Threshold Control for Near-Critical Systems

ABSTRACT (181 words)
Near-critical capacity-constrained systems operate close to their stability limits and are vulnerable to collapse after sequences of random replenishment disruptions. Although such failures are operational rather than physical, they create reliability-management challenges analogous to condition-based maintenance: intervention decisions must balance preventive costs against failure consequences. This paper develops a unified reliability framework for disruption-driven collapse in a stochastic inventory system with Poisson demand and random capacity interruptions. Collapse is modeled as a first-passage event. We show that the classical inverse-Gaussian diffusion approximation systematically overstates first-passage variance in the near-critical regime, and introduce an empirical correction for the inverse-Gaussian shape parameter that reduces mean absolute shape-parameter error from 36.0% to 5.0% across 42 calibrated design points. We then characterize the reliability hazard structure and show that decreasing-failure-rate behavior emerges through survivorship selection, making age-based intervention ineffective in key regimes. Building on these results, we formulate an average-cost Markov decision process and compute optimal condition-based threshold policies. Numerical experiments show cost reductions of up to 22% over the age-based optimum, stable thresholds under capacity variation, and regret below 6% under 30% demand-rate misspecification.

KEYWORDS
inventory systems; disruption management; Markov decision process; threshold policy; condition-based maintenance; first-passage reliability; diffusion approximation

HIGHLIGHTS
- Models disruption-driven collapse as a first-passage reliability problem.
- Corrected IG approximation cuts shape-parameter error from 36.0% to 5.0%.
- Direct hazard tests show approximate DFR across all feasible configurations.
- Threshold intervention reduces long-run cost by up to 22%.
- Optimal thresholds remain stable under capacity and demand-rate perturbations.
